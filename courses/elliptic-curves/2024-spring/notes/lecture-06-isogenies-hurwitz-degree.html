Lecture 6: Isogenies, Hurwitz, and Degree ​

Student notes: global PDF pp. 44–53
Date in notes: Mar 7, 2024
Theme: Nonzero origin-preserving maps between elliptic curves are finite unramified group homomorphisms.

Overview ​

This lecture studies morphisms between elliptic curves. The main objects are nonconstant maps

f:(E1,O1)⟶(E2,O2)

with f(O1)=O2.

Definition

An isogeny is a nonconstant morphism of elliptic curves preserving the origin.

The lecture proves three structural facts:

  1. an isogeny is finite and surjective;
  2. an isogeny between elliptic curves is unramified in the separable case;
  3. an isogeny is automatically a group homomorphism.

1. Morphisms of Projective Curves ​

Lemma

A nonconstant morphism

f:C1→C2

between irreducible projective curves is surjective and finite.

Proof. The image of a projective variety is closed. Since f is nonconstant, the image has dimension 1. A closed one-dimensional subset of an irreducible curve is the whole curve. Thus f is surjective. A surjective morphism of projective curves has finite generic fiber; properness then implies it is finite. ◻

Remark

The constant origin-preserving map is the zero homomorphism

P↦O2.

The interesting maps are the nonconstant ones.

2. Hurwitz Formula ​

Theorem

Let f:C1→C2 be a finite separable morphism of smooth projective curves. Then

2g(C1)−2=(deg⁡f)(2g(C2)−2)+∑P∈C1(eP−1),

where eP is the ramification index at P.

Corollary

Every finite separable morphism between elliptic curves is unramified.

Proof. For elliptic curves g(E1)=g(E2)=1. Substituting into Hurwitz gives

0=∑P∈E1(eP−1).

Each term eP−1 is nonnegative, so every term is zero. Hence eP=1 for all P. ◻

Remark

Student-note idea

The genus-one condition is extremely rigid. A finite separable self-map of an elliptic curve has no room for ramification because both canonical divisors have degree zero.

3. Pullback of Differentials ​

Let ωi be a nonzero invariant differential on Ei.

Proposition

For any isogeny f:E1→E2, there is a scalar cf such that

f∗ω2=cfω1.

Proof. The pullback of a regular differential is regular. Since

dim⁡H0(E1,ΩE1)=1,

f∗ω2 is a scalar multiple of ω1. ◻

Lemma

If f is separable, then cf≠0. If f is purely inseparable, the pullback on differentials can vanish.

Explanation. Separability means the induced extension of function fields has nonzero differential. In characteristic p, Frobenius sends functions to pth powers, so differentials can vanish because d(ap)=0.

4. Isogenies Preserve Addition ​

Theorem

Every isogeny

f:(E1,O1)→(E2,O2)

is a group homomorphism.

Proof. Use the Picard description of the group law. A point P∈Ei corresponds to

OEi((P)−(Oi)).

For P,Q∈E1, the equality P+Q=R means

(P)+(Q)−2(O1)∼(R)−(O1).

Push this divisor relation forward under f. Since f(O1)=O2, the resulting degree-zero divisor relation on E2 is

(f(P))+(f(Q))−2(O2)∼(f(R))−(O2).

By the Picard description on E2, this says

f(P)+f(Q)=f(R)=f(P+Q).

Thus f preserves addition. ◻

Attention

This proof is conceptual. It avoids checking the chord-and-tangent formulas directly.

5. Multiplication Maps and Torsion ​

Definition

For n∈Z, the multiplication map is

[n]:E→E,P↦nP.

The n-torsion subgroup is

E[n]=ker⁡[n].

Proposition

If chark∤n, then

deg⁡[n]=n2

and over an algebraically closed field

E[n]≅(Z/nZ)2.

Proof idea. The differential of [n] on invariant differentials is multiplication by n. If chark∤n, this is nonzero, so [n] is separable. The geometric kernel size equals the degree. The line-bundle calculation developed next gives deg⁡[n]=n2, hence the kernel has n2 points. Compatibility among torsion levels forces the group structure to be (Z/nZ)2. ◻

Example

For n=2 in a short Weierstrass equation

y2=x3+ax+b,

the nonzero 2-torsion points are exactly the points with y=0, i.e. the roots of the cubic. Together with O, this gives four points when the cubic has distinct roots.

6. Degree by Divisors ​

Definition

If f:C1→C2 is finite, its degree is

deg⁡f=[k(C1):k(C2)].

Proposition

For any point Q∈C2,

deg⁡f∗(Q)=deg⁡f.

Proof. Pullback of a point records the fiber over Q with ramification multiplicities. The sum of these multiplicities is the degree of the finite field extension. ◻

TIP

Lecture takeaway

Isogenies are simultaneously:

  • finite maps of curves;
  • homomorphisms of groups;
  • maps whose degree can be read from divisors;
  • maps whose separability is detected by differentials.
