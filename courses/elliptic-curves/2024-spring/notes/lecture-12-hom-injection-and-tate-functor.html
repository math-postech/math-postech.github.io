Lecture 12: The Hom Injection and the Tate Functor ​

Student notes: global PDF pp. 110–118
Theme: The Tate-module functor is faithful: a homomorphism of elliptic curves is detected by its action on Tate modules.

Overview ​

The main theorem is the injectivity of

Hom(E1,E2)⊗Zℓ⟶HomZℓ(TℓE1,TℓE2).

The proof recorded in the student notes has a concrete mechanism:

  1. if a map kills ℓm-torsion, it factors through [ℓm];
  2. if it kills all Tate-level data, it is divisible by all powers of ℓ;
  3. finite generation of Hom groups forces such an element to be zero.

1. The Natural Map ​

Proposition

Every homomorphism ϕ:E1→E2 induces a Zℓ-linear map

Tℓϕ:TℓE1→TℓE2.

Proof. For P∈E1[ℓn],

[ℓn]ϕ(P)=ϕ([ℓn]P)=0.

Thus ϕ(P)∈E2[ℓn]. Compatibility with the transition maps [ℓ] gives a map on inverse limits. ◻

This defines

Hom(E1,E2)→HomZℓ(TℓE1,TℓE2).

Tensoring with Zℓ gives the stated map.

2. Factorization Through Multiplication ​

Lemma

If a homomorphism

ϕ:E1→E2

kills E1[ℓm], then there exists a homomorphism λ:E1→E2 such that

ϕ=λ∘[ℓm].

Proof. The isogeny [ℓm]:E1→E1 has kernel E1[ℓm]. If ϕ kills this kernel, then ϕ is constant on the fibers of [ℓm]. Therefore ϕ descends to the quotient

E1/E1[ℓm].

But this quotient is canonically the target of [ℓm], again isomorphic to E1. Hence there is a map λ:E1→E2 with ϕ=λ∘[ℓm]. ◻

Remark

Student-note idea

This is the algebraic version of “a function constant on fibers factors through the quotient.”

3. Injectivity Before Tensoring ​

Theorem

The map

Hom(E1,E2)→HomZℓ(TℓE1,TℓE2)

is injective.

Proof. Suppose Tℓϕ=0. Then for every m, ϕ kills E1[ℓm]. By the factorization lemma,

ϕ=λm∘[ℓm]

for every m. Thus ϕ is divisible by ℓm inside Hom(E1,E2) for every m.

The group Hom(E1,E2) is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group. In such a group, the only element divisible by all powers of ℓ is zero. Hence ϕ=0. ◻

Lemma

If M is a finitely generated free abelian group, then

⋂m≥0ℓmM={0}.

Proof. Choose a Z-basis. If a nonzero vector has coordinates with greatest common divisor d, it cannot be divisible by ℓm for m>vℓ(d). ◻

4. Injectivity After Tensoring ​

Theorem

The scalar-extended map

Hom(E1,E2)⊗Zℓ↪HomZℓ(TℓE1,TℓE2)

is injective.

Proof. Let

x∈Hom(E1,E2)⊗Zℓ

map to zero. Choose a Z-basis ψ1,…,ψr of the finitely generated group Hom(E1,E2). Write

x=∑iaiψi,ai∈Zℓ.

Modulo ℓm, the vanishing of Tℓx implies that the corresponding integral combination acts trivially on E1[ℓm]. By the factorization lemma, that combination is divisible by ℓm in the Hom group. In coordinates, every coefficient is divisible by ℓm.

This holds for every m, so every ai=0 in Zℓ. Hence x=0. ◻

Attention

This proves faithfulness, not fullness. Not every Zℓ-linear map between Tate modules comes from an elliptic-curve homomorphism.

5. Why Surjectivity Is Harder ​

The student notes flag the missing condition: maps on Tate modules must respect additional structures.

At minimum, the Weil pairing imposes a symplectic constraint:

eℓ(Tℓϕx,y)=eℓ(x,Tℓϕ^y).

Over finite fields, the full Tate theorem says one must also impose Galois compatibility.

Theorem

Tate philosophy

Arithmetic homomorphisms of elliptic curves should be recovered from Galois-compatible homomorphisms of Tate modules.

TIP

Lecture takeaway

The injection lets us study endomorphisms as matrices:

End(E)⊗Zℓ↪M2(Zℓ).

The next step is to read trace and determinant from these matrices.
