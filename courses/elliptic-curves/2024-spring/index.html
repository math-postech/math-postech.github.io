Elliptic Curves ​

  • Grading: Problem sets
  • Main Reference: Silverman, The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, GTM 106

Schedule Notes ​

Lecture Notes ​

Lecture notes are reconstructed from student in-class notes and the instructor's preparation notes. They are organized by lecture, following the existing course-page style: each item has a title, source scope, and short content summary.

Foundations and Curves ​

  • Lecture 1: Functors, Schemes, and Projective Space — Categories vs sets, representable functors, affine schemes as functors, Zariski topology, and projective space as a functor of lines.
  • Lecture 2: Divisors, Line Bundles, and Picard Groups — Curves, divisors, rational equivalence, line bundles on curves and on P1, divisor-line bundle correspondence, Picard group.
  • Lecture 3: Riemann–Roch and Weierstrass Equations — Riemann–Roch, canonical divisors, differentials on P1, genus-one curves, construction of 1,x,y, and the Weierstrass equation.

Elliptic Curve Structure ​

  • Lecture 4: Group Law on Elliptic Curves — The identification C≅Pic0(C), point addition by tensor product of degree-zero line bundles, and chord-and-tangent geometry.
  • Lecture 5: Invariant Differentials — The canonical differential dx/(2y+a1x+a3), translation invariance, and uniqueness of global differentials.
  • Lecture 6: Isogenies, Hurwitz, and Degree — Nonconstant origin-preserving morphisms, Hurwitz formula, unramified maps, multiplication-by-n, and deg⁡[n]=n2.
  • Lecture 7: Theorem of the Cube and Line Bundles — Cube theorem, recurrence for [n]∗L, symmetric line bundles, and the bridge to heights.

Isogenies, Frobenius, and Tate Modules ​

  • Lecture 8: Isogenies and Group Structure — Cube theorem applications, determinant line bundles, pushforward by isogenies, and why isogenies preserve addition.
  • Lecture 9: Dual Isogeny, Weil Pairing, and Endomorphisms — Dual isogenies, positive definite pairing on homomorphisms, restrictions on End(E), and Weil pairing motivation.
  • Lecture 10: Finite Fields, Frobenius, and Point Counting — Frobenius fixed points, #E(Fq)=deg⁡(1−Frobq), Hasse bound, and zeta-function motivation.
  • Lecture 11: Tate Modules and the Weil Pairing — Inverse limits, E[ℓn], TℓE≅Zℓ2, finite-level Weil pairings, and the symplectic structure on Tate modules.
  • Lecture 12: The Hom Injection and the Tate Functor — Proof that Hom(E1,E2)⊗Zℓ injects into Tate-module homomorphisms; factorization through [ℓm] and divisibility.
  • Lecture 13: Trace, Determinant, Hasse Bound, and Supersingular Behavior — Trace/determinant on TℓE, characteristic polynomial, Hasse bound, Frobenius eigenvalues, and ordinary vs supersingular p-torsion.

Source Note ​

These notes combine two sources:

  2. the instructor's preparation notes, which supply motivation and intended structure.

The result is a reconstructed course text rather than a verbatim transcript.
