Discussion — Product of Two Positive Definite Matrices ​

Question ​

Let A,B be real positive definite matrices. Since AB is usually not symmetric, must the eigenvalues of AB still be positive?

A=AT>0,B=BT>0.

The answer is:

Yes. All eigenvalues of AB are positive real numbers.

There is no counterexample in any dimension, not only in 1×1 or 2×2.

Key Reduction ​

Use orthogonal diagonalization on one matrix. Suppose

A=QDQT,D=diag(d1,…,dn),di>0.

Conjugating the whole problem by Q, we may reduce to the case

A=D>0

diagonal positive, while B is still symmetric positive definite.

So the question becomes:

DBis not symmetric, but does it have positive eigenvalues?

The Algebraic Trick ​

Let

D1/2=diag(d1,…,dn).

Then

DB=D1/2(D1/2BD1/2)D−1/2.

Therefore

DB is similar to D1/2BD1/2.

But

C:=D1/2BD1/2

is symmetric, because

CT=D1/2BTD1/2=D1/2BD1/2=C.

It is also positive definite, because for every nonzero vector x,

xTCx=xTD1/2BD1/2x=(D1/2x)TB(D1/2x)>0.

So C is symmetric positive definite. Hence all eigenvalues of C are positive real numbers.

Since DB is similar to C, they have the same eigenvalues. Therefore all eigenvalues of DB, hence of AB, are positive.

Coordinate-Free Version ​

Let

S=A1/2.

Then

AB=S(SBS)S−1.

So

AB∼SBS.

The matrix SBS is symmetric positive definite. Therefore AB has positive real eigenvalues.

Interpretation ​

The product AB loses symmetry, but it does not lose positive spectrum, because it is only a disguised symmetric positive definite matrix.

The disguise is removed by the square-root conjugation:

AB⇝A1/2BA1/2.

This is the clean mechanism.
