Lecture 9: Dual Isogeny, Weil Pairing, and Endomorphisms ​

Student notes: begins around global PDF p. 75
Date in notes: Mar 19, 2024
Theme: Dual isogenies produce a positive degree form, which strongly restricts endomorphism algebras.

Overview ​

This lecture has three linked goals:

  1. construct and use the dual isogeny;
  2. turn degree into a positive definite quadratic form;
  3. explain why End(E) is highly restricted.

The Weil pairing enters as the torsion-level refinement of the same duality.

1. Dual Isogeny ​

Theorem

Let ϕ:E1→E2 be an isogeny of degree n. There exists a unique isogeny

ϕ^:E2→E1

such that

ϕ^∘ϕ=[n]E1,ϕ∘ϕ^=[n]E2.

Proof idea. Use the Picard identification E≃Pic0(E). Pullback gives

ϕ∗:Pic0(E2)→Pic0(E1).

Transporting this map through the identifications with E2 and E1 defines ϕ^. The norm-pullback identity for divisors gives

ϕ^∘ϕ=[deg⁡ϕ],

and symmetry gives the other composition identity. ◻

Remark

Idea

The dual isogeny is not an inverse unless deg⁡ϕ=1. It is an inverse up to multiplication by the degree.

2. Degree as a Quadratic Form ​

Definition

For ϕ,ψ∈Hom(E1,E2), define

⟨ϕ,ψ⟩=deg⁡(ϕ+ψ)−deg⁡ϕ−deg⁡ψ.

Proposition

This pairing is symmetric and bilinear after extending scalars to Hom(E1,E2)⊗Q.

Proof idea. The degree function satisfies a parallelogram identity because it comes from a symmetric line bundle and the cube theorem:

deg⁡(ϕ+ψ)+deg⁡(ϕ−ψ)=2deg⁡ϕ+2deg⁡ψ.

The polarization identity then gives a symmetric bilinear form. ◻

Theorem

The form ⟨−,−⟩ is positive definite on Hom(E1,E2)⊗R.

Proof. If ϕ≠0, then ϕ is an isogeny onto its image and has positive degree. Thus the quadratic form q(ϕ)=deg⁡ϕ is positive away from zero. The associated bilinear form is positive definite because q satisfies the parallelogram law. ◻

3. Endomorphism Algebra Restrictions ​

Proposition

The natural map

Z→End(E),n↦[n],

is injective.

Proof. If [n]=0 for n≠0, then deg⁡[n]=n2>0, contradiction because the zero morphism has degree 0. ◻

Theorem

The real algebra

End(E)⊗ZR

is isomorphic to one of

R,C,H,

where H is the Hamilton quaternion algebra.

Explanation. The positive definite degree form rules out nilpotents and indefinite behavior. The dual isogeny gives a positive involution. Finite-dimensional real division algebras with such positivity are restricted to R, C, and H.

Remark

In characteristic zero, only the commutative cases occur:

End(E)⊗Q=Qor an imaginary quadratic field.

In positive characteristic, the quaternionic case occurs for supersingular elliptic curves.

4. Differential Test for Separability ​

Lemma

Let ϕ:E→E′ be an isogeny. If

ϕ∗ωE′=0,

then ϕ is inseparable. If ϕ is separable, then ϕ∗ωE′≠0.

Proof. A separable map induces a nonzero map on differentials of function fields. A purely inseparable map in characteristic p behaves locally like u↦up, whose differential is zero. Since invariant differentials span the global differential space, the displayed condition detects the inseparable part. ◻

Remark

This is why Frobenius behaves differently from multiplication by ℓ≠p on Tate modules and torsion.

5. Weil Pairing Motivation ​

Definition

For n prime to the characteristic, the Weil pairing is a map

en:E[n]×E[n]→μn.

Theorem

The Weil pairing is bilinear, alternating, nondegenerate, and compatible with isogeny duals:

en(ϕP,Q)=en(P,ϕ^Q).

Proof idea. For P∈E[n], choose a divisor DP representing (P)−(O) and a rational function fP with

div(fP)=nDP.

Evaluating such functions against divisors representing the second torsion point gives a root of unity. Weil reciprocity proves independence of choices and bilinearity. Compatibility with ϕ^ follows from the pullback/pushforward relation for divisors. ◻

TIP

Lecture takeaway

Duality appears in three forms:

ϕ^∘ϕ=[deg⁡ϕ],deg⁡(ϕ) is positive,en(ϕP,Q)=en(P,ϕ^Q).
